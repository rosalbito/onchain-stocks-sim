How the book tracks Nasdaq


  

Two prices, and the bots in between

The book follows Nasdaq only because the LP keeps re-posting. Between a Nasdaq move and the LP’s re-post, its quote is stale: that gap is the race.

Two prices exist. Nasdaq’s is the true one; on-chain, nobody sees it directly. The on-chain book only knows orders: its “price” is the best bid and best ask, and the mid is their average. The book tracks Nasdaq only because the LP bot keeps re-posting around the price it last saw. Worked example: the LP has quoted around 100.00, so the book shows a bid at 99.99 for 10 and asks at 100.01 for 10 and 100.02 for 5. Nasdaq moves to 100.05. The sniper sees it in 5 ms, adds up every ask at or below 100.04, and sends one immediate-or-cancel buy for 15 shares with a limit of 100.04. It fills 10 at 100.01 and 5 at 100.02, unless the LP’s cancel, sent after the LP sees the move 20 ms late, lands first. One subtlety: the sniper’s limit takes any ask at or below it, including fresh LP quotes it hasn’t seen yet. With long blocks, the LP’s new quotes sit stale until their block seals, and a sniper order in the same block takes them. The regular trader never looks at Nasdaq; it just trades against whatever the book shows.
